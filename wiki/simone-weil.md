# Simone Weil

Weil was a philosopher. The post says she "wrote a lot about attention". The Stanford entry calls attention her central ethical concept and says it informs her later social and political thinking. The sources I have do not give her dates, so check them before this page goes live.

## Why it matters to this post

The post uses her in its meeting-agent example. The agent joins the meeting, records everything and produces a summary, so nobody needs to take notes. The post asks what else we give away, and answers that we give away the reason to listen properly.

The post says Weil believed paying attention was one of the most important things we can do. In the post's terms, listening is more than collecting information. It means:

- noticing things
- questioning them
- understanding why someone disagrees
- sometimes changing your mind

A summary gives you the information, but not the experience that led to it. That leads to the post's design question: should the agent simply summarise the meeting, or point to the biggest disagreements and the questions that still need human judgement?

## Doors out

- [Simone Weil](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/simone-weil/), Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. A scholarly entry that treats attention as her central ethical concept.
