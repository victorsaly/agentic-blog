## The idea

It bothers me. A company brings in a support agent. It answers customers at 3 a.m., in different languages. Customers are happy, money is saved. Everyone wins.

Two years later, nobody in the team really understands the product. The learning from mistakes went to the agent. When nobody understands what is happening, nobody decides, and the whole workflow is in jeopardy.

I think we ask the wrong questions. "Technology is neither good nor bad" is a convenient excuse. Nobody takes responsibility: not who built it, not who sold it, not who decided to use it. When you build an agent, you decide what people are used for, and what it will stop them doing.

Those decisions are older than computers. Aristotle said the purpose comes first: someone imagines drinking before they design the cup. Heidegger said that when we see everything as a resource, a customer becomes a conversion. And the agent will treat people as resources, if that is how we measure them.

So for every feature, write down what it gives and what it takes away. If you can't, perhaps you don't understand the feature yet.

This is not against technology. It freed us from hard, repetitive and dangerous work. I just want us to name the people affected, and make the trade-off a conscious decision.
