Every AI agent takes something away. Decide what, before you build it.

Picture a support agent. It answers customers at 3 a.m., in different languages. Customers are happy, money is saved.

Two years later, nobody in the team really understands the product. The learning from mistakes went to the agent. When nobody understands what is happening, nobody decides.

It bothers me. "Technology is neither good nor bad" is a convenient excuse. Nobody takes responsibility: not who built it, not who sold it, not who decided to use it.

When you build an agent, you decide what people are used for and what they will stop doing.

So for every feature, write down what it gives and what it takes away. If you can't, perhaps you don't understand the feature yet.

I am pro AI. I want to build more and push further. I just want us to be honest about the price, and to name the people affected before production. Changing the architecture later is harder than a conversation now.
