It bothers me.

A company brings in a support agent. It answers customers at 3 a.m., in different languages. Customers are happy. Money is saved. Everyone wins.

Two years later, nobody in the team really understands the product. The learning from mistakes went to the agent.

When nobody understands what is happening, nobody decides. The whole workflow is in jeopardy.

I think we ask the wrong questions. "Technology is neither good nor bad" is a convenient excuse. Nobody takes responsibility: not who built it, not who sold it, not who decided to use it.

When you build an agent, you decide what people are used for. You also decide what they will stop doing.

So for every feature, write down two things. What it gives. What it takes away. If you can't write the second one, perhaps you don't understand the feature yet.

Take the support agent. Does it close the ticket? Or does it prepare the answer, so a human learns from it and takes responsibility? That is a design decision, not an accident.

This is not against technology. It freed us from hard, repetitive and dangerous work. I am pro AI, and I want to build more.

I just want us to be honest about the price. Name the people affected before production. Changing the architecture later is harder than a conversation now.

Every agent takes something away. Decide what, before you build it.
